## Copy categories

No universal length requirement; these are NN/g's working definitions.

- **Long-form copy** — roughly **3+ paragraphs** perceived as one continuous
  unit. Made up of smaller elements (short-form, microcopy). Articles, reports,
  about-us pages.
- **Short-form copy** — roughly **2–3 paragraphs** on one idea or point.
  Product descriptions, mission statements, longer summaries.
- **Microcopy** — roughly **fewer than 3 sentences**. Titles, summaries, card
  content.

Microcopy can: **inform** (deliver important/interesting information),
**support interaction** (communicate available actions), **influence**
(encourage an immediate digital action).

## Informative microcopy

Primary goal is to inform. Examples: titles, headings, summaries, card content,
informative push notifications.

Helps users: scan pages and screens, find what they need, get important
information quickly, make decisions.

Can: encourage clicking/tapping/reading more, convince users the effort is
worth it, provide information scent.

### Relationship to long-form copy

Microcopy makes long-form easier to **scan**, **understand**, **navigate**.
Long-form supports microcopy with **details**, **comprehensive stories**,
**communication of complex ideas**. Common microcopy inside long-form: titles,
headings, lead paragraphs, summaries.

### Microcopy is a first impression — scanning patterns

- **F-pattern** — gazes trace an "F" (mirrored in RTL languages). Inefficient;
  usually means the user needed more informative microcopy elements.
- **Layer-cake pattern** — users focus on layers (headings). More efficient;
  users find information faster.
- **Commitment** — where people commit to reading a section. Related to
  layer-cake: informative microcopy directs them to where they commit.

### The 3 C's

1. **Clarity** — deliver key information
2. **Concision** — keep it short
3. **Character** — use your organization's brand voice

## Goal 1: Clarity

> **Information scent** — a user's estimation of the value of a source of
> information for their needs, based on a representation of that information
> source.

Assessed on two factors: the **usefulness** of the information, and the **ease**
with which it can be found.

> **Signposts** — cues that help users keep track of where they are in
> long-form copy and understand what information is available. E.g. titles,
> headings, and subheadings that indicate place and position.

Informative microcopy must:

- **Be relevant** — offer worth to the user and their task. Consider
  personalized, dynamic content where possible (e.g. Netflix adapting to a
  known account holder).
- **Avoid jargon** — use words the audience can reasonably understand. Jargon
  may be necessary for professionals. Where a term must stay but needs
  explaining, use a **tooltip**.

### Tooltip rewrite example

**Goal:** help someone pick a camera.

*Before*
> Still Image resolution indicates the maximum number of megapixels (MP) a
> camera sensor is able to capture. The higher the number of megapixels (MP),
> the greater the possible image resolution.

*After (options)*
> Megapixels (MP) indicate how sharp a picture will be. 48 MP is considered
> professional level while 20 MP is considered general use.

> Megapixels (MP) indicate how detailed your photos can be. Higher numbers offer
> more detail. 12–24MP works for most people taking photos for social media or
> personal use, while professionals usually work with more than 30MP.

### Editing for clarity

Identify: key takeaways for the user; goals of the long-form copy and the
microcopy; what would make it relevant (signpost — does it indicate what's in
the section? information scent — is it clear what it's about?); jargon to avoid.
Then align the microcopy to those targets.

With enough context, AI can help identify all of these. Sometimes microcopy
problems are long-form copy problems — consider each part of the whole.

### Writing for clarity — steps

1. Identify the **user's key takeaway** — be able to state it clearly.
2. Identify the **organization's goals** — what it wants emphasized; balance
   user and organizational needs.
3. Consider **visual design and information hierarchy** — important in
   structured UI elements. What needs to be read first? What's the priority?
   Where are users most likely to see it?
4. **Write** — choose the most plain and straightforward way to communicate.

## Goal 2: Concision

Concision = efficiency: users absorb the meaning as quickly as possible.
**Clarity > concision.** If extra words add clarity, it's usually okay.

> **Front-loading** — placing the most important, unique, information-carrying
> words at the beginning of the microcopy.

Avoids truncation problems; scanning users are more likely to find relevant info.

### Common English concision mistakes

**Expletives** — words/phrases that add nothing, usually
[impersonal pronoun] + [to be]: *There is/was/were/will be*. Rearrange to fix.
*There was an error in the code.* → *The code had an error.*

**Determiners and modifiers** — add specificity to a noun but become clutter
when they add nothing significant. Watch for `-ly` adverbs, *very*, *really*,
*kind of*. Delete to fix. *Usability tests are really the backbone of UX.* →
*Usability tests are the backbone of UX.*

**Passive voice** — the subject does not perform the action; the action is
performed upon them. *The book was placed on the shelf* vs *I placed the book on
the shelf*. Test: add "by zombies" to the end — if it makes sense, it's passive.
Find the subject and rewrite so it performs the action.

**Redundancy** — words or phrases repeating the same idea. *The personalized
individual interface.* Delete to fix; some cases require rearranging.
*This interface is intuitive and easy-to-use* → *This interface is intuitive.*

### Length suggestions

- **Titles:** 60 characters max
- **Email subject lines:** 60 characters max
- **Description:** 160 characters max
- **Push notifications:** about 200 characters max

Check: current platform or device requirements; your company style guide or
design system; CMS or available design-space limits. Ideal length depends on
situation, context, and content — focus on directness.

### Questions when editing for concision

- Can I reduce word count while preserving the message?
- Are there any common concision mistakes in this microcopy?
- Do any character limits apply?
- Is it front-loaded?

Generative AI is a good concision editing partner. Include in the prompt: the
microcopy, a quick description of the context, optionally a screenshot, and the
four questions above.

Informative microcopy can be longer if necessary for **clarity**, **SEO
keywords**, or **style constraints**.

## Goal 3: Character

> **Character** — tone of voice, references, humor, personality, branding,
> emojis, or anything else that creates a sense of uniqueness and connection in
> the writing. The most subjective C.

> **Tone of voice** — the personality or mood conveyed by the way something is
> written or spoken.

Character often causes a loss of clarity — use it carefully.

**Clarity > Concision > Character.** Don't include character at the cost of
clarity. Character must line up with the audience reading it; learn this through
research. Other types of microcopy might need more character, but for
informative microcopy it's only nice to have.

## Strategies

When planning informative microcopy, remember the 3 C's: *Clarity* — what
information is needed to be clear? *Concision* — what character/word limits
apply? *Character* — what tone should I use? Also consider free-form idea
generation. As you gain experience, you rely less on planning.

Editing makes sure the text achieves its strategic goals efficiently and
effectively. Editors are fresh eyes — the closest you'll get to someone
understanding users without testing. Use a checklist to support subjective
evaluation.

### AI

**Bad at:** replacing user research; creating outputs without context.
**Good at:** defining an existing tone; generating microcopy options and ideas;
summarizing long-form copy; reducing word count.

To provide context, include in the prompt: what you're writing, where it will
appear, who your audience is, what tone you're aiming for, what the message
should be, and what you want the AI to give you.

Hints: prepare to reiterate; ask for multiple options; have the AI evaluate the
options against your own criteria; take the best ideas from all options.

## Evaluation

Evaluate clarity through metrics and qualitative user research. Frequency
depends on audience and product. Evaluate: **informative microcopy strategy**
(microcopy expected to affect user perception, to inform future writing) and
**mission-critical microcopy** (biggest impact on user goals, business goals,
or both).

> **Proxy metrics** — indirect indicators that your informative microcopy might
> be working.

- **Incoming traffic** — clicks into the content; might indicate initial interest.
- **Bounce rate** — clicking in and leaving immediately; might indicate
  incorrect expectations or disappointment.
- **Time on page** — might indicate ability to hold attention.
- **Scroll depth** — might indicate ability to hold attention.
- **Support tickets** — might indicate tooltips or help text aren't guiding users.
- **Reduced errors** — might indicate microcopy is helpfully guiding users.

No metric tells you exactly why something is happening, or how users understand
or resonate with your copy.

### Usability testing

Meet one-on-one, give realistic tasks, watch them use the product or prototype,
ask follow-ups. Requires screensharing software, participants representing your
actual audience, and incentives.

Tips:
- Do **not** just point to your microcopy and ask people what they think.
- Give realistic tasks that organically expose them to the microcopy.
- Ask open-ended follow-up questions.
- Watch and listen for: hesitation, pauses, confused expressions, skimming vs
  reading, incorrect assumptions.
- Identify patterns across participants.
- Connect findings to the three goals: clarity, concision, character.

## The editing checklist (from the course deck, verbatim)

| Group | Criteria | Question | Answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| Clarify | **Key Takeaway** | Did the microcopy communicate the key takeaway? | Yes / No |
| Clarify | **Jargon + Brand Terms** | Can the audience reasonably understand the word choice? | Yes / No |
| Concision | **Clutter** | Have all unnecessary words been removed or rephrased? | Yes / No |
| Concision | **Front-Loaded** | Is the primary information contained in the first half of the microcopy? | Yes / No |
| Concision | **Character-Limits** | Does this fit the standard character limit for this microcopy? | Yes / No |
| Character | **Tone of Voice** | Does the tone of voice match the audience and brand? | Yes / No |
| Miscellaneous (For All) | **Vibe Check** | Does it sound good and/or natural? | Yes / No |
| Misc. *(omit for push notifications and subject lines)* | **SEO Keywords** | Does the microcopy include relevant keywords to improve searchability? | Yes / No / N/A |
| Misc. *(omit for push notifications and subject lines)* | **Design Appearance** | Does the microcopy visually align with the design? | Yes / No |

## Follow-up questions for informative microcopy (research)

| Goal | Follow-Up Question |
|---|---|
| **Clarity** | "In your own words, what was that [microcopy] telling you?" <br> "Was there anything that made this easy or difficult to understand?" |
| **Concision** | "Was there anything unnecessary or confusing in that [microcopy]?" |
| **Character** | "If this [microcopy] were spoken by a person, who would they be?" <br> "If you had to describe this [microcopy] in one word, what would it be?" |

## Practice scenarios (worked examples from the course PDFs)

### Practice 3 — Edit for Concision

**Task:** a company specializing in car safety features wants this card content
cut to a **60-character title** and **160-character summary** to fit design needs.
**Audience:** automotive professionals who purchase and implement safety
technologies — tech-savvy, looking for contractors who understand the car
industry as deeply as they do.

Original card (title / image / summary / "Learn More" button):

> **Title:** Innovative Automotive Safety Systems That Help Prevent Crashes and Save Lives
>
> **Summary:** We design cutting-edge automotive safety systems that help
> prevent collisions, protect passengers, and enhance driver awareness. From
> smart sensors to automated braking, our technologies are trusted by leading
> manufacturers to keep roads safer for everyone.

The deck supplies the **same four-question checklist twice — once for the title,
once for the summary** (reduce word count? common concision mistakes? character
limits apply? front-loaded?). The lesson in the structure: each element gets its
own pass.

### Practice 4 — Add Some Character

Adjust each sample along a **Less Character <-> More Character** scale. A red
circle marks where the original sits; a gold star marks the target. **The dial
moves both ways** — two of the four samples need *less*:

| # | Original | Original level | Target |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "This name must match your legal ID to avoid withdrawal delays." | low | **far higher** |
| 2 | "Time to refill your meds. Future You says thanks." | middle | **lower** |
| 3 | "Well, that didn't work. But neither did the Titanic, and people still talk about it." | far right (max) | **far left (minimum)** |
| 4 | "We're not in your area yet. Try changing your delivery address." | low-middle | **slightly higher (middle)** |

Sample 3 is the cautionary one: a joke inside a failure state is over-dialed.

### Practice 5 — Putting It All Together

Five steps: **Scenario -> Plan -> Write -> Edit -> Final Copy.**

**Task:** a large hospital network improving its site copy needs microcopy under
the **date-of-birth form field**, shown when patients try to change their
prescriptions. Testing showed users hesitate to enter their DOB, seeing it as
unnecessary since the hospital already has it; the copy must explain the DOB is
needed for **identity verification**.

**Audience:** all patients, from tech-savvy to limited digital literacy. Plain,
conversational language; no medical jargon or complex instructions. Tone warm,
respectful, supportive — focused on reducing stress and building trust.

**Limit:** no more than **80 characters**.

The mockup places the copy as hint text directly beneath the field:

```
Date of Birth*
+----------------------+
| DD/MM/YYYY           |
+----------------------+
[YOUR MICROCOPY HERE]
```

### Practice 6 — Start a Research Plan

- **Step 1** — pick a product: something you're actively working on, worked on
  recently, or use often.
- **Step 2** — explore it and identify informative microcopy. Where does it tend
  to appear? Are there areas with more of it than others? What types do you
  notice? Any concerns about how users might respond?
- **Step 3** — sketch a qualitative usability study. Who is the target audience
  (jobs, experiences, education levels, backgrounds)? What tasks would naturally
  lead participants to encounter and react to the microcopy? What follow-up
  questions would answer your research questions (see the table above)?
